An Instagram playbook for brands

Your brand page has become a family WhatsApp group.

Everyone's in it. Hardly anything in it is meant for them. So they mute it. This playbook walks you through six decisions to turn one crowded page into a small media network that each audience actually wants to follow.

00 · The idea

The fix is one page per interest

The internet splits people by what they love. The narrower a page's audience, the better Instagram can find the right people for it, and the more those people engage.

Chefs, places, habits

    Decision 02

    Which interests deserve their own page?

    Think about what your audience loves next to your product. Luggage leads to travel stories. Food delivery leads to chefs. An events app leads to comedy and movies.

    Infographic · Map your audience first

    Draw your buyers as one big circle, then the interests inside it. This example is for a luggage brand.

    • Big bubble, little overlap (airport humour): a good candidate for its own page.
    • Heavy overlap (packing hacks and travel stories): keep them together as two series on one page.
    • Small bubble (work trips): a recurring series, not a page.

    Then score each bubble with the cards below.

    Size · how many people care about it here? Passion · do they save and share? Closeness · could a fan become a customer? Repeatable · could you make 100 episodes? Open space · are strong pages missing?

    Decision 03

    Which structure fits?

    Four models, simplest first. Move to the next one only when the current one is working.

    Model A

    One sharp brand page

    One page, organised into two or three named series so it doesn't feel like a noticeboard.

    Use when
    One core audience, frequent purchases, small team.
    Watch out
    It slides back into the family-group problem as you add products.
    Model B

    Brand page + one content page

    The brand page sells. A separate entertainment page keeps people close between purchases.

    Use when
    People buy rarely and share one strong interest.
    Like
    Mokobara's micro-drama page.
    Watch out
    The content page turns into a disguised ad and loses its audience.
    Model C

    Hub + interest pages

    A brand hub plus one page per interest group, each with its own voice.

    Use when
    Several distinct audiences with little overlap.
    Like
    District, Nike, Netflix.
    Watch out
    A thin team leaves every page half alive.
    Model D

    Category media brand

    Lightly branded pages about the whole category that grow into shows others pay to appear on.

    Use when
    You can staff a studio and want sponsorship revenue.
    Like
    Swiggy's "Served".
    Watch out
    Slow payback. Leadership needs 12+ months of patience.

    How visible should your brand be on the new page?

    Fully branded

    Same name, logo and colours, like "Brand Comedy".

    Pick it when your brand already has fans in that interest.

    Endorsed

    Its own name and look, with "by Brand" in the bio.

    The usual default. Feels independent but still credits you.

    Independent

    Its own name. Your brand shows up only in credits or collabs.

    Pick it when your logo would put viewers off, or you plan to sell sponsorships.

    Decision 04

    Which shows will people come back for?

    A format is a show, not a post. Pin up one card like this for every series before you film anything.

    Series nameSomething a fan could say out loud.
    One-line premise"Every week, ___ tries ___."
    Recurring faceA host or character people recognise.
    The hookWhat happens in the first two seconds.
    Length and shapee.g. a 45-second Reel: setup, twist, payoff.
    CadenceSame days every week. Consistency beats volume.
    Product's roleNone, background or a cameo. Never the plot.
    Success signalRewatches, shares and "part 2?" comments.

    Four quick tests for a format

    • Would someone with no interest in your product watch it to the end?
    • Can you list 20 episode ideas today?
    • Would a fan notice if an episode went missing?
    • Is it easy to send to one specific friend?
    Decision 05

    How will you run and measure it?

    Every page needs an owner, a rhythm and a rule for when to stop.

    Ownership

    One named owner per page, even if the production team is shared.

    Cadence

    3 to 5 Reels a week per interest page, on fixed days.

    Cross-promotion

    Collab posts and the odd mention. No sales posts on entertainment pages.

    Voice

    Shared values and limits. Tone, look and humour set per page.

    Review rhythm

    Weekly per format, monthly per page, quarterly for the whole network.

    Stop or merge

    Flat after 90 days of steady posting? Change the format once, then fold it back in as a series.

    Judge each layer by different numbers

    Brand page

    Track

    Profile visits, link clicks, saves on product posts, conversions.

    Don't judge by

    Viral reach alone.

    Interest or content page

    Track

    Watch time, completion, shares, reach beyond followers, follower growth.

    Don't judge by

    Sales in the first six months.

    Whole network

    Track

    Total engaged audience, cost per launch, inbound collab requests, sponsorship revenue.

    Don't judge by

    Any single page's numbers.

    Decision 06

    How does it pay you back?

    The returns come in stages. Know which step you're on, and don't expect step five in month two.

    Infographic · The media-network flywheel

    Each turn of the loop makes the next one easier. Good shows keep people watching. Watching earns reach. Reach builds an audience you own. That audience makes launches cheaper and attracts partners who pay, and that money funds more shows.

    The loop starts slowly. Most brands give up during the first turn, before reach catches up with effort.

    1. RelationshipPeople know and like your pages. Sign: comments asking for the next episode.
    2. Cheaper launchesNew products land with a warm audience. Sign: launch posts beat paid ads on cost per sale.
    3. CollaborationsBrands, creators and celebrities want in. Sign: collab requests you didn't ask for.
    4. SponsorshipOther companies pay to reach your audience. Sign: a paid integration that doesn't hurt retention.
    5. Revenue streamThe network becomes a business line. Sign: media revenue covers its own costs.

    Hold off on a network if…

    • You haven't made one page work yet. Fix the format before multiplying it.
    • You can't commit 6 months of steady posting to each new page.
    • Your audiences overlap a lot. Use named series on one page instead.
    • Leadership expects sales from entertainment pages within a quarter.
