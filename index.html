    Which audiences deserve their own pages?

    Which audience interests are worth building pages around?

    Think about what your audience loves next to your product. Luggage leads to travel stories. Food delivery leads to chefs. An events app leads to comedy and movies.

    Infographic · Map your audiences before creating new pages

    Draw your buyers as one big circle, then the interests inside it. This example is for a luggage brand.

    • Big bubble, little overlap (airport humour): a good candidate for its own page.
    • Heavy overlap (packing hacks and travel stories): keep them together as two series on one page.
    • Small bubble (work trips): a recurring series, not a page.

    Then swipe on to score each bubble.

    How to score an interest

    Rate each interest from 1 to 5 on five questions

    Size · how many people care about it here? Passion · do they save and share? Closeness · could a fan become a customer? Repeatable · could you make 100 episodes? Open space · are strong pages missing?
    Scores out of 25:20–25: launch a dedicated page15–19: test it as a recurring series firstUnder 15: don’t invest in it yet

    Which Instagram structure fits your team?

    Choose the right Instagram structure for your team

    Four options, simplest first. Move to the next one only when the current one is working.

    Option A

    One page with focused content series

    One page, organised into two or three named series so it doesn't feel like a noticeboard.

    Accounts
    Best forOne core audience, frequent purchases, small team.
    AudienceOne core audience, organised by named series.
    Team neededWorks even with under 3 posts a week.
    Link to your brandIt is your brand page.
    ExampleMost early-stage D2C brands.
    Watch outIt slides back into the family-group problem as you add products.
    Option B

    Your brand page plus one entertainment page

    The brand page sells. A separate entertainment page keeps people close between purchases.

    Accounts
    Best forPeople buy rarely and share one strong interest.
    AudienceBuyers on the brand page; one shared interest on the entertainment page.
    Team neededAt least 3 to 7 posts a week.
    Link to your brandBranded, endorsed or independent (see below).
    ExampleMokobara's micro-drama page.
    Watch outThe content page turns into a disguised ad and loses its audience.
    Option C

    One main page plus pages for different interests

    A brand hub plus one page per interest group, each with its own voice.

    Accounts
    Best forSeveral distinct audiences with little overlap.
    AudienceOne page per interest group, each with its own voice.
    Team needed1 to 2 dedicated people or more.
    Link to your brandBranded, endorsed or independent (see below).
    ExampleDistrict, Nike, Netflix.
    Watch outA thin team leaves every page half alive.
    Option D

    Build a media brand around your industry

    Lightly branded pages about the whole category that grow into shows others pay to appear on.

    Accounts
    Best forYou can staff a studio and want sponsorship revenue.
    AudienceEveryone interested in your whole category.
    Team neededA content team, plus 12+ months of leadership patience.
    Link to your brandLightly branded.
    ExampleSwiggy's "Served".
    Watch outSlow payback. Leadership needs 12+ months of patience.

    What content will people return for?

    What recurring content will make people follow and return?

    A format is a show, not a post. Pin up one card like this for every series before you film anything.

    The show
    What will the audience call this show?Something a fan could say out loud.
    What happens in every episode?"Every week, ___ tries ___."
    Who or what will viewers recognise?A host or character people recognise.
    Each episode
    What grabs attention in the first two seconds?What happens in the first two seconds.
    How will each episode unfold?e.g. a 45-second Reel: setup, twist, payoff.
    How often will new episodes appear?Same days every week. Consistency beats volume.
    The product and the proof
    How will the product appear without becoming the whole story?None, background or a cameo. Never the plot.
    What will show that people want more?Rewatches, shares and "part 2?" comments.
    Example

    "60-second fixes": a recurring explainer series. One host, one problem, one fix, every Tuesday and Friday.

    Test your content idea before you invest in production

    • Would someone with no interest in your product watch it to the end?
    • Can you list 20 episode ideas today?
    • Would a fan notice if an episode went missing?
    • Is it easy to send to one specific friend?
    Decision 055 of 6

    How will you run and measure each page?

    How to run each page and know whether it’s working

    Every page needs an owner, a rhythm and a rule for when to stop.

    Assign one accountable owner to every page

    One named owner per page, even if the production team is shared.

    Set a posting schedule your team can maintain

    3 to 5 Reels a week per interest page, on fixed days.

    Decide how your pages will promote each other

    Collab posts and the odd mention. No sales posts on entertainment pages.

    Give each page its own voice within your brand guidelines

    Shared values and limits. Tone, look and humour set per page.

    Set weekly, monthly and quarterly reviews

    Weekly per format, monthly per page, quarterly for the whole network.

    When to fix, pause or merge an underperforming page

    Flat after 90 days of steady posting? Change the format once, then fold it back in as a series.

    Measure each page by the results it is designed to deliver

    Measure your brand page by business results

    Track

    Profile visits, link clicks, saves on product posts, conversions.

    Don't judge by

    Viral reach alone.

    Measure content pages by audience growth and engagement

    Track

    Watch time, completion, shares, reach beyond followers, follower growth.

    Don't judge by

    Sales in the first six months.

    Measure the combined value of your Instagram network

    Track

    Total engaged audience, cost per launch, inbound collab requests, sponsorship revenue.

    Don't judge by

    Any single page's numbers.

    How will the network pay off?

    How an Instagram network creates business value over time

    The returns come in stages. Know which step you're on, and don't expect step five in month two.

    Infographic · How content, audience growth and revenue reinforce each other

    Each turn of the loop makes the next one easier.

    1. 1Good shows keep people watching.
    2. 2Watching earns reach.
    3. 3Reach builds an audience you own.
    4. 4That audience makes launches cheaper and attracts partners who pay.
    5. 5That money funds more shows, and the loop starts again at 1.

    The loop starts slowly. Most brands give up during the first turn, before reach catches up with effort.

    1. Build audience loyaltyPeople know and like your pages. Sign: comments asking for the next episode.
    2. Lower customer acquisition costsNew products land with a warm audience. Sign: launch posts beat paid ads on cost per sale.
    3. Attract collaborationsBrands, creators and celebrities want in. Sign: collab requests you didn't ask for.
    4. Earn sponsorship revenueOther companies pay to reach your audience. Sign: a paid integration that doesn't hurt retention.
    5. Build a media business that pays for itselfThe network becomes a business line. Sign: media revenue covers its own costs.

    When you shouldn’t create more Instagram pages yet

    • You haven't made one page work yet. Fix the format before multiplying it.
    • You can't commit 6 months of steady posting to each new page.
    • Your audiences overlap a lot. Use named series on one page instead.
    • Leadership expects sales from entertainment pages within a quarter.

    A plain-English guide for founders and clients

    Why one Instagram page isn't always enough

    When one brand page tries to talk to everyone, most followers see posts that aren't for them, so they stop paying attention. Some brands fix this by running a few pages, each made for one kind of follower. This short guide helps you decide whether that makes sense for you.

    The idea in 30 seconds

    Think of it like a family WhatsApp group

    1. 1Everyone is in it, but most messages aren't for you. So you mute it.
    2. 2Smaller groups about one thing people love (comedy, movies, food) get read.
    3. 3Your brand page still does the selling. The other pages keep people interested in between.

    Your options

    The three usual setups

    Most brands end up with one of these. Start with the simplest one that fits.

    Keep one page, organised

    Split your posts into two or three regular series, so followers know what to expect.

    Good if your followers mostly want the same things.

    Add one fun page

    Your brand page sells. A second, entertaining page keeps people thinking about you between purchases.

    Good if people buy from you rarely, like luggage or furniture.

    A few pages for different interests

    Your main page plus one page for each big group of followers, each with its own style.

    Good if different people follow you for very different reasons.

    Very large brands sometimes go further and build a whole media brand about their industry, like Swiggy's "Served". That needs a full content team and at least a year of patience.

    What it takes

    Before you add a page, check you can keep it going

    One owner per page

    A named person responsible for each page, even if they share a team.

    3 to 5 short videos a week

    Per extra page, posted on the same days every week. Steady beats a lot at once.

    About 90 days of patience

    Give a new page 90 days before deciding. Judge it on whether people watch and share, not on sales in its first six months.

    What you get back

    How it pays off, step by step

    The benefits come in this order, and they take time. Don't expect step five in month two.

    1. People know and like your pagesThey comment asking for the next episode.
    2. Launches cost lessNew products reach people who already like you, without big ad spend.
    3. Others want to work with youBrands and creators start asking to collaborate.
    4. Sponsors pay to reach your audienceOther companies pay to appear on your pages.
    5. The pages pay for themselvesMoney from the pages covers what they cost to run.
    Before you say yes

    Questions to ask your team or agency

    If someone proposes a new Instagram page, these five questions tell you whether it's ready.

    1. Who exactly is this page for?And do they really want different posts from our main page?
    2. What's the recurring show?Could we list 20 episode ideas today?
    3. Who owns it, and how often will it post?A named person, on fixed days each week.
    4. How will we judge it in the first 90 days?Watch time and shares, not sales.
    5. What happens if it isn't working?Change the show once, then fold it back into the main page.
